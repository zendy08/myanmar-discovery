04 — Culture & people

What the photographs cannot hold.

A few living things, set beside one another — not a catalogue.

Faith, in public

The day begins with bowls.

Before the heat, monks walk the lanes. Rice is given, not as spectacle — as breakfast, and as merit.

The face of the country

Thanaka

Bark, ground on stone with a little water. Worn on cheeks against the sun — cream, cool, faintly of sandalwood.

The table

Mohinga, then tea.

Catfish broth and rice noodles at morning. Later, pickled tea leaves tossed with nuts, garlic, a little heat — laphet thoke.

On the water

Work as a kind of poise.

At Inle the oar is wrapped around the leg. It looks like dance. It is how you keep both hands free for the net.

Cloth

The longyi

A sheet of cotton or silk, knotted at the waist. Ministers wear it. Boatmen wear it. Acheik silk still comes off Amarapura looms.

April · the new year

Thingyan

Water, thrown with intent: to wash the old year off. Streets become rivers. Elders are sprinkled, not soaked, if you know the manner.

05 — Good to know

A few things, before you go.

Practical, not exhaustive. Open only what you need — the country will teach the rest.

Most passports need an eVisa before flying. Apply through the official immigration site; keep six months’ validity on the passport. Rules shift — read them close to your date, not from an old blog.

Shoes and socks off at the gate. Shoulders and knees covered. Sit so the soles of your feet do not point at a Buddha, or at an elder. Ask before you photograph someone at prayer. Do not touch a monk’s robes.

Yangon, Mandalay, Bagan, Inle — think in hops, not a single road. Domestic flights save days. The Ayeyarwady between Mandalay and Bagan is the slow, right way if you have the hours. In towns: taxis, and walking in the cool.

Day-to-day is kyat, in cash. Cards are not a plan. If dollars are asked for, they should be new, unmarked, uncreased. Check current travel advice before you pack — this is a living country, not a museum hour.
